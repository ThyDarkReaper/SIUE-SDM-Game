For uncomplicated pseudomembranous oral candidiasis, first-line therapy is topical antifungal treatment...
...with nystatin oral suspension being the most commonly recommended initial agent.
Although clotrimazole troches are also an effective topical option, they rely on adequate salivary flow to dissolve properly and distribute the medication across the oral mucosa.
In patients with severe xerostomia, troches may not dissolve effectively, resulting in suboptimal drug delivery and reduced efficacy.
Therefore, a liquid suspension that can be actively swished to coat the mucosa is preferred in this clinical setting.
Systemic azoles (e.g., fluconazole) are reserved for refractory or severe cases.
